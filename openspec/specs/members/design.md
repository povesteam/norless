# Members design

Why members, roles, invitations, avatars and presence work as the spec says.

## Decisions

- **Three roles that combine**: owner, editor and team. Team covers the people at the laptop, the singers and the musicians, since all of them control live and change playlists. Owners pass every role check. Roles are about permissions only; the roles people play in a service (vocals, sound, host) are `team-schedule`'s and give no permission.
- **One check, on the server**: `requireRole` loads the member's roles for the community from the session, answers 401 without a session and 403 without the role, and every write route calls it first. Hiding controls in the interface is only a convenience.
- **Reading stays public**: reading, searching, exporting and projecting locally need no login, as in the old app. Changes, member lists and presence need a member with the right role. Screen devices see only live content, through their own secret URL or code.
- **Names go only to members**: song creators, playlist viewers, who is online, who changed the slide, who is recording, who leads. Stage screens open to visitors show the same things without the name. Avatar ids reach the browser only where names already do, so one rule keeps visitors from seeing people.
- **The team edits music, not words**: team and editors send chords, bar lines, `!` notes, notation blocks, tempo and reference links through one endpoint. The server accepts a text only when its lyric rows, section names and other lines are the same as before (leading spaces of a lyric row don't count, since they only align chords). So the check is made on the server, not trusted from the client. Lyrics, titles and the rest stay with editors.
- **No approval queue for the team's edits**: a change shows for everyone at once, as a revision with the author's name. Editors and owners restore a revision, and a restore is itself a save, so it can be undone.
- **Recordings belong to whoever made them**: others on the team see that a recording exists and ask for access, and its owner grants or refuses. A community owner may delete one but doesn't get to listen without a grant, since voices are personal data. A recording made from a laptop or a guest's phone belongs to the member who logged that device in, because the device's session ends.
- **Dislike reasons are for owners**: members see the counts and their own choice, and owners see each reason with the name. Only current members' opinions count, and deleting an account deletes its opinions. Excluding a song is an owner's call, with a reason.
- **The church calendar's address is a secret**: it is often a private Google Calendar link, so it is stored on the community but left out of the community data every page loads. Only owners get it, from the calendar's own route.
- **Practice rooms: the team starts, anyone joins**: the starter or an owner can switch a room between rehearsal and service, and end it. Its name is the starter's, and its link page shows no names to visitors.
- **Settings are tabs with their own addresses**: an owner can link to or reload `/settings/<tab>`. Members are managed there, with no menu entry of their own. On a phone, `/settings` lists the sections and each opens on its own page.
- **Destructive actions sit in a row's ⋯ menu, after a confirmation**: a red Remove on every row, or a trash icon on every slot, puts them where a slip hits them. One shared menu component serves them all, its button named "Actions: <name>". The trash buttons on a person's own lists (away days) stay.
- **An invitation is accepted by logging in**: an owner invites an email with roles, and the first login with that email, through Google or a link, makes the person a member. Nothing else has to be tracked, and the login proves the person owns the address. An existing account with that email, such as an imported one, is the one invited.
- **Invitation links last 48 hours**: an invitation may wait a while to be read, so its login link works once within 48 hours. Ordinary logins use 15-minute links. The email goes out in the owner's interface language. The owner sees whether it was sent; with no email set up, the page says the person can log in at `/login` with that address.
- **The first owner comes from the command line**: someone with access to the server runs the invite command once, since nobody can invite an owner before one exists. It matches an imported account by email.
- **Imported accounts are reviewed one at a time**: the old app had about fifty people, some inactive for years. No imported person can log in until invited, and no old password is kept. The old admin status is shown to help the choice but gives no role, since the old admin toggle was likely broken; the owner picks the roles when inviting. Deleting removes the email and name and leaves what the person made, without a name. An invited person is linked to their old account by email, so past songs and playlists show them as creator.
- **A community always has an owner**: the last owner can't be removed, can't drop their own owner role, and can't delete their account until they hand the role over.
- **Deleting an account erases the personal, anonymizes the shared**: name, email, preferences, photo, sessions, ideas, opinions, feature requests, away days, notifications and push subscriptions go, the laptops and guests they logged in go, and their schedule slots open again. Songs, playlists, usage events and the change log stay, without their name.
- **Attribution is recorded everywhere and shown sparingly**: every song, playlist, entry and live change records who made it. "Added by" shows only with the selected entry, not on every row. A song's creator and last editor show as small avatars with the name in a styled tooltip (no browser tooltip) and, beside them, as a sentence. Changes from a laptop name the member who approved it ("Laptop (Maria)"), so a shared device is still accountable.
- **Avatars are copied, not linked**: the member's photo, else initials. Norless keeps each photo as a 256-pixel square JPEG and serves it itself, so no browser contacts Google to show one. Avatars show at 56 pixels at most, and 256 covers a screen at 4x. Google's picture address has its size suffix rewritten to ask for 256, and ffmpeg crops the middle. A year's cache is safe because a replaced photo gets a new id.
- **Photo source is remembered**: google, own or initials. An uploaded photo or initials stop Google's from coming back. Google's address is kept even then, so "Use my Google photo" works at once. A job every 6 hours, and at server start, copies Google's photo again when the copy is a week old, so a changed photo follows without a login. A photo Google no longer serves keeps the last copy and is tried again after another week. The address goes with the account.
- **A photo never blocks a login**: the Google login waits at most 2 seconds for the copy, so the first page shows the photo, and a failure is logged and skipped.
- **Photo upload is for everyone logged in, at every feature level**: it is a privacy control like deleting one's account, and a member must always be able to remove a Google photo. The browser shrinks a phone's photo to 512 pixels first; the server accepts only JPEG, PNG and WebP up to about 2 MB and makes the square. A replaced or deleted photo is deleted, so Norless keeps one per person at most.
- **Devices have no photo of their own**: a laptop or a guest's phone acts as the member but isn't them, so the server refuses (403) a photo change from it, and My account hides the section.
- **Presence is following a topic**: being online is following the community's presence topic, which only members may do. Each connection is a device of its person, told phone or computer by the User-Agent, which is good enough for a label.
- **Who's here is avatars only, names in a tooltip**: a row of avatars is short enough for a playlist's title row. The tooltip (hover, keyboard focus, long-press) reads name, devices and "last live change", and is also the avatar's accessible name. The last live change is a ring that is always drawn, transparent unless it applies, and the row keeps an avatar's height while loading, so nothing moves.

## Rejected

- Separate operator and musician roles: they differed only in changing playlists.
- Holding the team's chord changes for approval: a rollback from the history covers a mistake.
- Showing Google's photo by its address: Google would see every page view that shows an avatar.
- Names, device icons or an online dot beside each avatar in who's here: avatars alone keep the row short, and the tooltip carries the rest.
- A red Remove button on every member row: a slip hits it.
