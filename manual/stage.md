# On stage

For the musicians and singers. Once switched on, the community's bar has
**Instruments**, **Vocals** and **Stage monitor**. Each follows what's live, by itself.

## Instruments

![The Instruments view on a phone](../screenshots/phone-musicians.png)

- **Bar grid** (the default): the whole song as rows of bars, the live part highlighted
  and the next one marked.
- **Chords over words**: the live part with its chords, and the next part's chords.

Both show the key and the time signature. A song without chords shows its words and
its structure, and the team can add the chords from there.

On **My account**, under **Music**, list your instruments and mark your main one; it
chooses your layouts:

- **Guitar:** the capo and the shapes to play, from the shapes you like (C, A, G, E,
  D). Change the capo for a song on your device; the shapes follow.
- **Keys:** the chords in concert pitch, or as numbers.
- **Drums:** the parts by bars, with a big cue for the next one.
- **Bass:** the roots.

Chords can read as letters, Do-Re-Mi or numbers, for you or for the whole community.

**Chord colors** (My account, Music; the ⓘ beside Plain chords says it in short): each chord in a color for its degree in the
key, so G in D looks like B♭ in F. A chord shares its color with its relative (C and
Am, F and Dm, G and Em), the minor one softer; the home chord stays plain, and chords
outside the key are magenta. A suffix (sus4, 7, maj7, dim) has its own shade, mixed
with its chord's color. A bass note after the slash takes its own degree's color.

![Chord colors](../screenshots/phone-chord-colors.png)

In the bar grid, a part's bars line up in rows, one per line of the song when they
don't fit on one. Two chords in a bar share it by their beats, which the Chords mode
sets.

## Vocals

![The Vocals view on a phone](../screenshots/phone-vocalists.png)

- **Whole song:** every part in order, scrolling by itself to the live part.
- **Sideways:** for a phone on a microphone stand, the live part and the next one.
  Held upright, the next part goes under the live one.
- **Tablet:** every part in two columns, in one language or both.

If you're in the team, a tap on a part sends it live: when the laptop falls behind,
the band can catch up from the stage. Scrolling doesn't send anything.

## Stage monitor

![The stage monitor](../screenshots/screen-stage-monitor.png)

The TV at the front of the stage: the live part and the next one whole, with their
notes, the key, the next song and a clock. During the sermon (a divider with planned
minutes), it counts down the minutes left. On a phone held upright, the next part goes
under the live one.

## Messages to the stage

The live bar has **To the stage:** "5 minutes", "Wrap up", "Time", or your own short
text. It shows on the stage monitor and over the musicians' and singers' parts, until
you clear it. The projectors never show it.

## Keys for a service

The team can set a song's key for one service on its playlist entry: the musicians'
chords, the stage monitor and the projector's key line follow, and the song itself
stays as it is. **Change key** on a song's page moves it for good, chords included.

## Tempo, recordings, notation

- **Listen for tempo:** one device in the room listens (its microphone, or a feed from
  the mixer). When the band drifts more than 4% from the song's tempo for 8 seconds,
  the stage shows "speeding up" or "dragging".
- **Record:** the team records a rehearsal or a service from any device. Norless cuts
  it into a file per song. A recording is yours; others ask you for access.
- **Notation:** short ABC blocks for an intro, a riff or a groove, written in the Chords
  mode, shown and played on the keys and drums layouts.
