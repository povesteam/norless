# Songs

Everyone can read the songs. Editors write and correct them; the team adds chords
and tempo.

![A song's page](../screenshots/laptop-song-page.png)

## Editing

The pencil on a song (or **Edit song** on its page) opens the editor: the title and
text per language, the key, the time signature and the tags. **Save** keeps it;
**Close** asks first when something isn't saved. Once switched on, the song's history
lists every save, and editors can restore an older one.

![The song editor](../screenshots/laptop-song-editor.png)

## Writing a song's text

Blank lines separate the slides. Everything else is the words as they are sung.

| Write | It means |
|---|---|
| `1:` or `2:` alone on a line | The verse below is verse 1, 2… |
| `R:` | The refrain. `R` alone later sings it again. |
| `[bridge]` | A part with any name. |
| `.G      C` | Chords for the line below, over the letters where they stand. |
| `. \| G \| C D \|` | Bar lines between the chords. |
| `/: … :/` | The lines between are sung twice; `/:.  .:/` three times. |
| `! unison` | A note for the singers and musicians, never projected. |
| `! drums: rim` | A note for one group of players. |
| `*` at the end of the last line | The final mark on the last slide. |

Unnamed slides are numbered among the verses by themselves.

Projectors leave out chords and notes. When a slide is too long to read, the editor
says which one: split it with a blank line, or shorten its lines.

## Chords mode

The team and editors can add chords without touching the words: tap a letter and pick
a chord, or play it on a MIDI piano. The tempo is typed or tapped there too.

Under a line with bar lines, each bar of two chords or more has a slider: drag it (or
use the arrow keys) to where the first chord ends, a step per beat. "| C G |" in 4/4 is
two beats each until you move it.

## Credits and recordings

Editors can note the authors, the copyright and the page a song was taken from. The
team can link a recording to learn it from ("Listen on youtube.com").

## Watch again

When the services are streamed on the church's YouTube channel (its address in
**Settings → Schedule**), Norless finds each service's stream within the hour after it.
A song's page then lists **Watch again**: the services it was played in, each opening the
stream where the song began. A past playlist's entries have **Watch in the stream** in
their menu, and the statistics link each most played song's latest service. When the
stream started earlier or later than Norless thinks, type when the first chapter starts
in the playlist's **YouTube chapters** and copy them: every link moves with it.
